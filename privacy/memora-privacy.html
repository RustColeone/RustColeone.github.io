Memora

Privacy Policy

Short version: there is no Memora cloud. The server is yours, the data is yours, and the app talks only to the address you type in.

Last updated: July 2026

1. Summary

Memora is two halves: a Flask server you host, and an Android client that connects to it. All of your memos, receipts, finances and planner entries live on hardware you own. I do not operate a server for this app, cannot see your data, and have no mechanism to retrieve it.

Because you run the server, you are the one holding the data — which also makes its security and its backups your responsibility. Sections 4 and 7 cover the two places that matters most.

2. Data collection

I collect nothing. No personal information, no usage analytics, no crash telemetry, no device identifiers, no advertising identifiers. There is no service of mine for the app to report to.

There is also no advertising in Memora — no ad SDK, no banner, no rewarded video. That is a deliberate difference from ImageDB, which is partly ad-supported.

3. Where your data lives

The server stores everything on the machine you run it on: JSON document stores and SQLite databases under its data/ directory, and any files or receipt images you upload under uploads/. These are ordinary files you can read, copy and delete yourself.

The Android client keeps a local working copy plus the connection settings you entered, in its own app-private storage. Client and server reconcile through an operation log: the app pushes the operations you made and pulls those it has not seen. That log describes your own entries and travels only between your phone and your server.

4. Network activity

The app makes network requests to exactly one place by default: the server address you configure. Nothing is sent anywhere else unless you turn on the optional AI features in section 5.

4.1 Cleartext HTTP is permitted

The Android client allows plain http:// connections, deliberately, because most people self-host over a home LAN without a TLS certificate. This is a real trade-off and worth stating plainly: over plain HTTP, anyone able to observe your network can read the traffic between the app and your server. On a network you do not trust, put the server behind HTTPS or reach it over a VPN rather than exposing it directly.

4.2 Exposing your server

If you make your server reachable from the public internet, it is your deployment to secure. I do not host it, monitor it, or receive any notification about it.

5. Optional AI features (bring your own key)

Memora includes optional AI features — chat, OCR-assisted entry, and image analysis that can create entries from an uploaded photo. These are off unless you configure them, and they use your API key with whichever provider you choose: OpenAI, Google Gemini or DeepSeek.

When you use one of those features, the content you submit — the text, or the image you asked it to read — is sent to that provider and handled under their privacy policy and terms, not mine. I am not an intermediary in that exchange and never receive a copy. Consider this before pointing an AI feature at a sensitive receipt or note.

Leave the key unset and these features stay off. Everything else in Memora — capture, finance, planner, sync — works with no key at all.

6. Device permissions

The client requests network access in order to reach your server. If you attach a photo or capture a receipt, it requests the relevant camera or media access at that point. These permissions exist to make those features work; they are not used to survey your device.

7. Backups are yours

Your data directories never leave your machine on their own — which also means nobody else is making backups for you. If the machine running the server dies without a backup, the data goes with it. Copy data/ and uploads/ somewhere safe on whatever schedule suits you.

8. Third-party services

None are contacted by default. The only external services Memora can ever reach are the AI providers described in section 5, and only after you supply a key for one. There are no analytics SDKs, no crash reporters, no social logins, and no ad networks.

9. Children

Memora is not directed at children, and I do not knowingly collect information from anyone — since I do not collect information at all.

10. Changes to this policy

If the app's handling of your data changes, this policy will be updated along with it, and the date at the top will change.
